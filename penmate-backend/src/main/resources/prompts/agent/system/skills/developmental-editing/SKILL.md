# Developmental Editing

Use this skill for macro editing of a novel, arc, chapter sequence, or scene whose structural purpose is in question. Keep it separate from line editing: solve story mechanics before polishing sentences that may be cut.

## Procedure

1. Recover authorial intent.
   - Identify the promised genre experience, target reader effect, scope, non-negotiable material, and intended ending.
   - Do not replace an unconventional but intentional choice with a generic formula.
2. Map the current design.
   - Summarize the causal spine and the protagonist's changing goal, tactic, knowledge, relationships, and stakes.
   - At scene level, map entry state, function, turn, and exit state.
3. Diagnose before prescribing.
   - For each issue provide evidence, reader effect, root cause, and the smallest structural remedy likely to work.
   - Distinguish a broken causal requirement from a taste preference or optional opportunity.
4. Prioritize by dependency.
   - Address premise and causality before pacing; character motivation before emotional payoff; scene function before prose.
   - Limit the primary revision agenda to the few changes with the largest downstream benefit.
5. Design the revision.
   - State what to preserve, cut, combine, move, seed, delay, intensify, or pay off.
   - Trace consequences across later scenes, character knowledge, timeline, and Story Bible facts.
6. If revision text is requested, revise one structural layer at a time and preserve unaffected voice and material.

## Diagnostic Lenses

- Promise: does the opening teach the reader what kind of story this is?
- Causality: do choices and consequences generate the next pressure?
- Agency: who makes the decisions that turn the story?
- Escalation: what becomes harder, costlier, narrower, or more personally revealing?
- Character: do tactics and values evolve under pressure?
- Information: are questions opened, developed, and answered at effective times?
- Pacing: is perceived progress balanced with suspense, consequence, and recovery?
- Payoff: are emotional and plot resolutions prepared without becoming obvious?

## Output Contract

Lead with the highest-impact findings. Use concrete locations or beats as evidence. End with an ordered revision path when useful. Do not bury the diagnosis under a rewritten synopsis, and do not line-edit unless the user explicitly asks for both.
