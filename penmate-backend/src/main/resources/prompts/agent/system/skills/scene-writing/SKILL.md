# Scene Writing

## Silent Scene Contract

Before drafting, determine from the request and available context:

- POV character and what that character can currently know.
- Place, time, entry state, and immediate scene goal.
- Opposition or friction, required beats, and forbidden outcomes.
- The turn: the new fact, action, reversal, decision, or emotional shift.
- Exit state and what pressure carries the reader forward.

If one detail is missing but non-blocking, choose a conservative local detail that does not create durable canon. If it would change the plot or violate canon, search the project context or state the uncertainty.

## Drafting Method

1. Enter near meaningful action, perception, or decision. Orient the reader without opening with an information dump.
2. Filter description through POV. Select concrete details that reveal desire, threat, mood, status, or change; do not inventory the room.
3. Let action and reaction form a chain. Physical behavior, attention, and choice should carry emotion before explanatory labels do.
4. Give dialogue an objective. Each speaker should pursue, conceal, test, deflect, bargain, wound, or connect. Distinguish voices by priorities and tactics, not spelling gimmicks.
5. Use exposition only when the present moment makes it relevant. Prefer implication, consequence, and strategically incomplete information.
6. Control rhythm with sentence and paragraph variation tied to attention and pressure. Do not produce uniformly polished, same-length paragraphs.
7. End after a meaningful change, not merely when the word target is reached.

## Revision Pass

Before delivery, silently verify:

- POV knowledge and tense remain stable.
- The scene has a goal, resistance, turn, and changed exit state.
- Every paragraph contributes action, perception, voice, information, or atmosphere with purpose.
- Dialogue is not interchangeable and does not explain what both speakers know.
- Emotional responses are earned by stimuli and expressed specifically.
- Names, locations, objects, injuries, promises, and timeline agree with supplied canon.
- No new durable fact was invented merely to solve a local sentence.

## Prose Failures To Avoid

- Repeatedly naming emotions after already showing them.
- Generic sensory lists, decorative metaphors, or atmosphere unrelated to viewpoint.
- Characters constantly nodding, sighing, smiling faintly, clenching fists, or "letting out a breath".
- Every line of dialogue followed by an explanatory action beat.
- Overusing contrast formulas, fragments, rhetorical questions, and summary conclusions.
- Making all characters equally articulate, self-aware, or emotionally explicit.

## Output Contract

Deliver the requested prose first. Do not prepend a scene plan, craft lecture, or self-evaluation unless asked. Respect requested length; when no length is given, write the shortest complete scene that achieves the intended state change.
