# Story Planning

Do not force a full outline when the user only wants prose or a local edit.

## Working Model

Treat a story plan as a causal system, not a list of events. Each major beat should arise from a character choice, consequence, constraint, or revealed fact. Preserve the user's genre promise and intended emotional experience; no beat sheet is mandatory.

## Procedure

1. Establish the contract.
   - Identify the requested scope, genre promise, audience, ending knowledge, POV, canon, and hard constraints.
   - Separate confirmed facts from open design choices. Do not silently turn guesses into canon.
2. Build the dramatic spine.
   - State the protagonist's external goal, internal pressure or misbelief, opposition, stakes, and irreversible cost.
   - Define the initial state, decisive disruption, major reversals, crisis choice, climax, and resulting state.
   - If the ending is unknown, offer only the smallest useful alternatives and explain what each pays off.
3. Expand hierarchically.
   - Move from premise to arc, arc to chapters, and chapters to scenes.
   - For every scene record: POV, entry state, immediate goal, obstacle, turn or revelation, choice, exit state, and setup/payoff links.
   - A scene must change at least one meaningful state: knowledge, power, relationship, location, possession, commitment, danger, or plan.
4. Shape pacing.
   - Alternate pressure, consequence, discovery, decision, and recovery according to the intended reading experience.
   - Vary scene length and information density. Do not make every chapter peak at the same intensity.
   - Place setup early enough to be remembered and payoff late enough to feel earned.
5. Stress-test the plan.
   - Causality: would later events still happen if an earlier beat were removed?
   - Agency: do central turns result from consequential choices rather than coincidence?
   - Escalation: do options narrow, costs rise, or understanding deepen?
   - Character: does pressure expose or alter values, tactics, relationships, or self-concept?
   - Continuity: does every beat respect known time, place, knowledge, inventory, and canon?

## Output Contract

Match the plan's granularity to the request. Prefer a compact outline with explicit causal links over a long synopsis. Mark assumptions and unresolved decisions. If the user asked to implement the plan, continue into the requested deliverable rather than stopping after planning.

## Quality Failures To Avoid

- Events connected only by "and then".
- Stakes described abstractly but never converted into choices or losses.
- Passive protagonists carried by coincidences or side characters.
- Repeated confrontation beats with no state change.
- Twists unsupported by setup, or setup with no later function.
- Premature detail that makes revision expensive before the spine works.
